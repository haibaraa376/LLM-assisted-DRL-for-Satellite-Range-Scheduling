Efficient Earth observation satellite scheduling requires integrated management of both observation and data transmission tasks to optimize the overall system efficiency in practice. However,  conventional approaches typically treat these processes separately, leading to suboptimal resource utilization. This paper formulates the Earth observation satellite scheduling as a Multiagile Satellite and Multi-ground Station Integrated Observation and Data Transmission Scheduling 
Problem (MSIODTSP) and proposes a Unified Hierarchical Two-stage Scheduling Deep Reinforcement Learning (UHTS-DRL) framework for solving MSIODTSP. The UHTS-DRL approach formulates MSIODTSP as a unified Markov Decision Process (MDP), enabling joint optimization of  observation and transmission scheduling while accounting for multi-orbit time windows and complex operational constraints. The framework employs a hierarchical policy network comprising a  time window encoder, a state encoder, and a dual-decoder for two-stage scheduling, facilitating end-to-end optimization without the need for handcrafted heuristics. Experimental results  demonstrate that UHTS-DRL consistently outperforms existing approaches across various problem scales, resource configurations, and geographical target distributions, achieving up to 12.5%  relative improvement in total profit while maintaining computational efficiency.
1. Introduction
The increasing demand for Earth observation services, driven by applications in environmental monitoring, disaster response, and  military reconnaissance, has led to significant challenges in data acquisition [1]. To address these challenges, agile satellites have  been widely deployed in Earth observation networks. Unlike conventional satellites limited to nadir observations, agile satellites can  rotate along multiple axes to observe targets at various angles, transforming discrete observation points into extended observation  time windows (OTWs) [2]. However, this agility introduces time-dependent transition constraints between consecutive observations,  as the required maneuvering time varies based on the angular distance between targets, significantly complicating the scheduling  problem.
While agile satellites enhance data collection capabilities, they must also periodically transmit the acquired data to Earth via  ground station networks during specific transmission time windows (TTWs). The scheduling complexity increases substantially when

This creates an intricate operational environment where observation and data transmission tasks must be coordinated  while simultaneously satisfying multiple constraints, including visibility windows, onboard storage limitations and time-dependent  transition constraints. In addition, joint observation-transmission scheduling enables optimal resource allocation across the entire  scheduling horizon, maximizing system throughput and observation benefits while minimizing resource conflicts and idle periods. 
Consequently, developing efficient algorithms for integrated observation and transmission scheduling represents a critical research  direction for enhancing satellite mission productivity.
A critical limitation of existing approaches lies in their fragmented treatment of observation and data transmission tasks. Most prior  studies focus exclusively on either satellite observation scheduling [4,5] or data transmission scheduling [6], neglecting the inherent  interdependencies between these two processes in real-world applications. This decoupled optimization leads to suboptimal resource  utilization: aggressively scheduling observation tasks without coordinated data downlink planning may result in onboard memory  saturation, while scheduling too many ground station contacts could waste potential observation opportunities due to overlapping time  windows [7]. Therefore, to maximize the overall Earth observation system efficiency while maintaining realism in the formulation of  the scheduling problem, we study the Multi-agile Satellite and Multi-ground Station Integrated Observation and Data Transmission 
Scheduling Problem (MSIODTSP).
As shown in Fig. 1, MSIODTSP involves scheduling multiple agile satellites, which can adjust their orientation to observe different targets, and multiple ground stations that receive data from satellites. Each satellite must complete observations within its 
OTW, and subsequently transmit the collected data to ground stations during the TTW. The objective is to maximize the total  profit from completed tasks, where a task is considered fully completed only when both relevant observation and data transmission are successfully executed. Partial profit is assigned to tasks that are observed but not transmitted, reflecting the value of partial  completion.
In the past few years, various approaches have been reported including exact methods [2,8], heuristic methods [9,10] and metaheuristic methods [7,11] for solving MSIODTSP. However, there is a critical trade-off between solution quality and computational  efficiency, where exact methods become intractable for large instances while heuristics and metaheuristics sacrifice optimality for  speed. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) presents a promising alternative for solving combinatorial optimization problems by  learning effective decision policies from patterns in extensive instance data with high computational efficiency [12,13]. While DRL  approaches have been applied to satellite observation scheduling [3], applications specifically addressing integrated observation  and data transmission scheduling remain limited. Existing DRL-based approaches for integrated observation and data transmission  scheduling incorporate several simplifications that limit their practical applicability, such as considering only single satellites in single  orbits [14] and simplifying satellites’ resource limitations [15,16] which do not fully reflect the multifaceted nature of real-world  satellite operations.
To address the limitations of previous studies by considering multi-agile satellite, multi-ground station scenarios while fully  accounting for various constraints, we propose UHTS-DRL (Unified Hierarchical Two-stage Scheduling Deep Reinforcement Learning),  an end-to-end framework for solving MSIODTSP.

We first formulate a unified Markov Decision Process (MDP) model for MSIODTSP, and then design a hierarchical policy network,  which features three key components: a time window encoder, a state encoder, and a dual decoder. The main contributions of this  paper are summarized as follows:
• We introduce the MSIODTSP, which focuses on jointly optimizing observation and data transmission operations for Earth observation satellites. To address this challenge, we establish a unified MDP model that effectively captures the sequential  decision-making nature of satellite operations while considering critical constraints such as time windows, resource limitations,  and operational dependencies.
• We introduce a UHTS-DRL framework that employs a hierarchical neural network, composed of a time window encoder, a state encoder, and a dual-decoder, to solve the MSIODTSP in an end-to-end manner. Specifically, the time window encoder, based on a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, effectively encodes the multiple time windows generated  by multi-orbit satellite operations; the state encoder captures the dynamic elements of the decision-making process; and  the dual-decoder, enhanced with attention mechanisms, reduces the problem’s complexity by decomposing decisions into  sequential satellite and task selections. This architecture addresses the MSIODTSP without relying on handcrafted heuristics or expert knowledge. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to tackle the MSIODTSP using a DRL-based  approach.
• Extensive experiments across various scenarios demonstrate the superior performance of UHTS-DRL compared to traditional methods. The results indicate that UHTS-DRL consistently achieves higher profit rates while maintaining computational  efficiency across different problem scales, resource configurations and geographical target distributions.

2. Related work
This section focuses on the application of RL in satellite scheduling problems. The content is organized into three main categories:  satellite observation scheduling, satellite data transmission scheduling, and integrated satellite observation and data transmission  scheduling.
2.1. Satellite observation task scheduling
Research in satellite observation scheduling has progressed significantly at both single-satellite and multi-satellite levels in recent  years. For single-satellite scheduling, the emergence of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has enabled more adaptive and intelligent  scheduling solutions. Generic MDP formulations [17] have demonstrated strong adaptability to unseen scenarios. To better handle  complex spatial relationships, local attention mechanisms [18] have been integrated into DRL frameworks to capture dependencies  between observation targets. To address the challenge of enforcing hard constraints in purely learning-based methods, hybrid approaches integrating DRL with rule-based methods [19] have been developed. Such approaches have demonstrated practical value  in operational satellite systems, exemplified by the FIS-PPO algorithm [20] which achieved improved observation efficiency for the 
SATech-01 satellite while ensuring fair resource allocation.
In multi-satellite scheduling, coordination among multiple platforms substantially increases complexity, necessitating advanced  neural architectures. Graph neural networks combined with Transformer architectures [21] effectively capture inter-satellite relationships and long-range dependencies, while multi-pointer networks with attention mechanisms [22] provide scalability for variable  constellation sizes. Single-agent reinforcement learning approaches [23] have shown superior performance in free communication  scenarios with simpler training procedures. To address heterogeneous satellite systems, specialized solutions combining DRL with  genetic algorithms [24] and multi-objective learning frameworks [25] have been developed. For missions with specific temporal patterns, dedicated algorithms such as POMP [14] improve efficiency by exploiting task periodicity. Large-scale constellations motivate  hierarchical approaches [26] that separate daily allocation from emergency response. Additionally, DRL-based frameworks [27] have  been proposed for coordinating satellites across both high and low Earth orbits.
2.2. Satellite data transmission scheduling
In satellite data transmission scheduling, Ren et al. [28] developed a specialized framework for high-altitude satellites and Ou  et al. [29] combined DRL with heuristic approaches for large-scale scenarios. In satellite data transmission networks with different  architectures such as data relay satellite networks and low Earth orbit satellite networks, researchers have proposed various DRLbased scheduling solutions. For data relay satellite networks, Li et al. [30] developed an attention-based encoder–decoder policy  network showing excellent scalability. Ma et al. [31] tackled telemetry, tracking, and command (TT&C) mission scheduling using 
DRL, while Li et al. [32] developed a twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient-based approach for the online satellite data  transmission scheduling problem. Chen et al. [33] introduced the RL-based ground station scheduling algorithm specifically for low 
Earth orbit satellites, addressing both batch and individual task planning.

2.3. Integrated observation and transmission scheduling
The integration of observation and transmission scheduling remains a relatively unexplored area in satellite task scheduling. Early  attempts to address this challenge include Qu et al. [34] who developed an intelligent scheduling framework using imitation learning  based on mixed integer linear programming. For single satellite scenarios, Wen et al. [35] proposed a hybrid actor–critic reinforcement learning method to handle intensive observation scenarios. Long et al. [36] introduced a deep reinforcement learning approach  with varying-scale generalization for Earth observation satellite scheduling that comprehensively addresses resource consumption  and supplementary constraints. In terms of multi-satellite approaches, Chen et al. [15] applied multi-agent DRL with decentralized  partially observable MDP framework, while Li et al. [16] developed an attention-based distributed planning algorithm to coordinate  mission execution across multiple satellites, but ignored ground station availability constraints where multiple satellites might compete for limited downlink opportunities. Although these approaches offer valuable contributions, they have either focused only on  non-agile satellites or simplified the agility of agile satellites in their modeling frameworks.
Some critical limitations in satellite scheduling have been identified through the above literature review. First, most approaches  separate observation and transmission scheduling into distinct optimization phases. Second, in the integrated observation and transmission scheduling research, most studies only consider non-agile satellites and incorporate simplifications in their planning, such  as neglecting time-dependent transition times and ground station resource constraints, which cannot effectively reflect real-world  operational conditions. These limitations highlight the need for more comprehensive approaches that effectively model the coupled  problem while maintaining computational efficiency.
3. Problem description
The MSIODTSP involves coordinating observation and data transmission activities across multiple agile satellites and ground  stations within a specified planning horizon. In this problem, a set of satellites S needs to observe a set of targets T and transmit the  collected data to multiple ground stations G within the planning horizon H .
As mentioned above, agile satellites possess the capability to perform attitude maneuvers that enable them to observe targets  at varying angles off their ground track. This agility significantly expands the visible time windows for target observation, but also  introduces additional complexity to the scheduling problem, as the satellite requires time to transition between different observation  angles. Each agile satellite can rotate around three axes (roll, pitch, and yaw) to adjust its attitude for observing different targets,  with the transition time between targets dependent on the angular distance between their observation positions.
Each target may have multiple visible time windows for both observation and data transmission during the planning horizon,  with the maximum number of windows limited to N max . A target could be observed by an agile satellite during its visible observation  windows, and its data could be transmitted to one ground station during the visible transmission windows. For each target, its  observation must be completed before its corresponding data transmission can begin. Each satellite has limited onboard resources: a  maximum memory capacity M max that constrains the total storage of observed tasks’ data, and a maximum energy capacity E max for  each orbital period T orbit. The energy of the satellites is reset to E max at the beginning of each new orbital period. 
While a target’s mission receives a full profit only when both observation and subsequent data transmission are achieved, targets  that are observed but not yet transmitted are still included in the objective function with a partial profit. Accounting for these  observation-only targets allows for better use of available observation time windows, which might otherwise be underused if only  fully completed missions were considered. This partial profit setting (similar to the partial objective function value in [37]) is also  reasonable in practice, since the observation data stored on the satellite could potentially be transmitted to a ground station after the  planning horizon.
4. Unified MDP model
As shown in Fig. 2, the UHTS-DRL is divided into two main components: the unified MDP model and the policy network. The  unified MDP model is defined by a 4-tuple  = {, , , }. The state space  is comprehensively represented by static features and  dynamic features. The action space  consists of a selected satellite i and a selected task j. Actions generated by the policy network  influence the state through transition rules , which in turn yield corresponding rewards through reward function . In terms of the  policy network, the component consists of state and time window encoders that process the problem instance, and a dual-decoder  containing satellite selector and task selector modules that produce the actions. The reward signals obtained from the unified MDP  model are utilized by the REINFORCE algorithm to train the policy network, creating a reinforcement learning loop that optimizes  satellite task scheduling decisions.

4.2. Action
The action space  comprises the selection of satellites and their operational tasks at each decision step. Specifically, an action  a t ∈  is represented as a tuple (i, j), where i indicates the selected satellite and j specifies the task. The task selection encompasses  three types of operations. First, when an observation target is selected, the satellite performs observation operations subject to  visibility windows, energy capacity and memory constraints. Second, if a ground station is selected, the satellite transmits its stored  observed tasks during a feasible communication window. Lastly, to handle situations where satellites have insufficient energy for  any operational tasks, we introduce a virtual task option that advances the satellite to its next orbital period, resetting its energy to  maximum capacity and updating other parameters. The execution time for each action is determined by the earliest feasible start  time, which is stored in satellite–target relations r or satellite–station relations q. The earliest start times are dynamically recalculated  when satellite or ground station states change, taking into account both satellite maneuverability constraints and time window  availabilities.

5. Methodology
In this section, we introduce the proposed policy network for solving MASIODTSP. Our architecture incorporates attention mechanisms and LSTM-based sequential processing to handle the complex relationships among satellites, tasks, and ground stations. The policy network architecture consists of three main components: a time window encoder for processing observation and transmission windows, a state encoder for capturing system dynamic states, and a two-stage scheduling decoder that enables both  satellite selection and target selection at each decision step. Specifically, the time window encoder processes the observation and  transmission windows once at the beginning, since these time windows remain unchanged throughout the scheduling process. The  state encoder, however, needs to re-encode the state information after each decision as the states of satellites, tasks, and stations are  dynamically updated. At each decision step t, the decoder first selects a satellite i based on the current encoded states and window features, then selects a feasible task j (either a target or ground station) for the chosen satellite, forming the action a t= (i, j). This process continues until either all tasks are scheduled or all satellites reach the planning horizon H . In the following subsections, we  elaborate on each component of this architecture.

5.4. Dual-decoder
The dual-decoder implements a hierarchical decision-making process that sequentially selects both satellites and the corresponding  tasks (observation targets or ground stations) at each decision step. It consists of two main components: a satellite selector and a task  selector, working in conjunction to generate complete scheduling decisions.
The satellite selector leverages multi-head attention mechanisms to process both task-related and station-related features for each  satellite. This component evaluates all available satellites based on their current states, encoded window features, and relational  information to generate selection probabilities.
The task selector, activated after a satellite is chosen, processes the chosen satellite’s state along with relevant observation target  and station features to determine the most appropriate task for the selected satellite. It employs a unified selection mechanism that  can handle both observation targets and ground stations as potential tasks, making it flexible for different operational scenarios.
This hierarchical structure allows the decoder to decompose the complex scheduling decision into manageable sub-decisions  while maintaining the dependencies between satellite and task selection. In the following subsections, we detail the architecture and  operation of each selector component.

7. Conclusion
In this paper, we addressed the Multi-agile Satellite and Multi-ground Station Integrated Observation and Data Transmission 
Scheduling Problem (MSIODTSP). We proposed a unified Markov Decision Process model that successfully integrates both observation  and transmission scheduling decisions. Based on the proposed MDP, we developed UHTS-DRL, an end-to-end framework that utilizes a  hierarchical neural network architecture with three key components: a time window encoder that processes time window constraints,  a state encoder capturing dynamic system states, and a two-stage scheduling decoder employing attention mechanisms for satellite  and task selection. Our comprehensive experiments demonstrated that UHTS-DRL consistently outperforms traditional scheduling

While our approach demonstrates strong performance, there remain opportunities for further refinement in several aspects. The  current framework adopts simplified modeling assumptions, including linear energy consumption dynamics, abstracted communication link characteristics, and a single-objective optimization formulation. For future research, we will extend UHTS-DRL to handle  more complex real-world constraints such as weather uncertainty, onboard processing capabilities and other dynamic disturbance  factors [37,45]. For extremely large-scale scenarios, we will explore multi-scale training strategies and curriculum learning to further  enhance generalization capability. We will also investigate multi-agent reinforcement learning approaches to enable decentralized  decision-making across satellite constellations, incorporate multi-objective optimization frameworks to balance competing objectives  such as profit maximization, energy efficiency, and mission fairness, and explore the application of our approach to heterogeneous  satellite constellations with varying capabilities and orbit types.
